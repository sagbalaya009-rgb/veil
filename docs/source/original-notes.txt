android-permissions.md

Android permissions and platform feasibility

Version: 1.0

Status: Platform feasibility analysis

Priority: Critical

Executive summary

Sentinel is technically feasible on Android.

However, it depends on a combination of Android APIs that are heavily regulated by Google.

The product is possible.

The challenge is not engineering.

The challenge is platform policy.

The architecture must therefore be designed to satisfy both Android technical constraints and Google Play policy requirements.

The key question

Can an Android app monitor screen content in real time and intervene across apps?

The answer is:

Yes, with important limitations.

The required capabilities are available through:

- MediaProjection

- Accessibility Service

- Overlay windows

- Foreground services

These APIs are legitimate Android features.

The risk is how they are used.

Required permissions

Accessibility Service

Purpose

Observe foreground app changes.

Detect UI events.

Support intervention behavior.

Assist navigation during protection.

MediaProjection

Purpose

Capture screen frames.

Run on-device AI analysis.

Detect explicit content.

Overlay permission

Purpose

Display the intervention screen above other applications.

Notification permission

Purpose

Protection status.

Cooldown completion.

Service persistence.

Accessibility Service analysis

Accessibility is the most sensitive permission.

Google Play requires that accessibility services be used for legitimate accessibility or user-facing assistance purposes.

Sentinel’s justification

The service helps users enforce self-control commitments through real-time intervention and lock-period enforcement.

The service provides a user-facing protective function.

The accessibility service must not:

- collect text unnecessarily,

- record user activity,

- transmit screen content,

- monitor users secretly.

Accessibility implementation rules

Only observe events necessary for:

foreground app detection,

intervention timing,

lock enforcement,

permission recovery.

Do not perform OCR.

Do not read message contents.

Do not log accessibility events.

MediaProjection analysis

MediaProjection allows screen capture with explicit user consent.

This is the core technical capability required for Sentinel.

The user must actively approve screen capture.

The approval is visible.

Android displays a screen capture indicator.

This is beneficial because it reinforces transparency.

MediaProjection limitations

The permission can be revoked.

Some OEMs modify behavior.

Performance varies by device.

Future Android versions may change restrictions.

The monitoring architecture must tolerate interruptions.

Overlay analysis

Sentinel requires a full-screen overlay for immediate intervention.

Android allows overlays through:

SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW

Requirements

Transparent permission request.

Clear user explanation.

Easy revocation.

The overlay should appear only during interventions.

It should not remain permanently visible.

Foreground service analysis

Continuous monitoring requires a foreground service.

Android increasingly restricts background services.

A foreground service with a persistent notification is the most reliable approach.

The notification should clearly indicate:

Protection active.

This improves transparency and compliance.

Sentinel risk register

Version: 1.0

Status: Strategic risk assessment

Priority: Critical

Executive summary

Sentinel is an unusual product.

It combines:

- screen monitoring,

- AI vision,

- background services,

- behavioral intervention,

- and strong privacy guarantees.

This creates significant technical, platform, legal, and trust risks.

The purpose of this document is to identify the risks that could realistically cause Sentinel to fail and define mitigation strategies before development begins.

Risk methodology

Each risk is evaluated on:

- Probability

- Impact

- Detectability

- Mitigation

Medium

High

Sentinel founder memo

Version: 1.0

Confidential

Why this company exists

I do not believe the biggest problem with smartphones is distraction.

I believe the biggest problem is the growing gap between intention and behavior.

People increasingly know what they want to do.

Study.

Sleep.

Exercise.

Build.

Create.

Pray.

Read.

Protect their relationships.

Avoid pornography.

Avoid doomscrolling.

Avoid wasting hours online.

The problem is that knowledge is not enough.

The phone is optimized for the opposite.

Every swipe, recommendation, autoplay sequence, notification, and infinite feed is designed to reduce friction.

The result is that people repeatedly violate commitments they made to themselves.

Sentinel exists to protect those commitments.

The insight

Most blockers fail because they try to control access.

Sentinel tries to protect intention.

That sounds subtle.

It is not.

Blocking a website is different from interrupting a behavioral spiral.

A user rarely plans to spend three hours consuming explicit content.

They usually enter a sequence.

Boredom.

Scrolling.

Curiosity.

Escalation.

Compulsion.

Regret.

The opportunity is the escalation window.

A one-second intervention at the right moment is worth more than a thousand blocked websites.

The product

The first product is simple.

Sentinel watches the screen locally.

When explicit content appears,

it interrupts the session.

The screen goes black.

A cooldown begins.

A recovery action starts.

The user is separated from the impulse.

That is the entire product.

Everything else is secondary.

Why privacy is the moat

The obvious reaction is that screen monitoring is terrifying.

That reaction is correct.

Which is exactly why privacy must become the moat.

Sentinel should be architected so that the company cannot see what users see.

No screenshots.

No uploads.

No cloud inference.

No screen history.

The AI has eyes but no memory.

That phrase should become the foundation of the company.

Trust is not marketing.

Trust is architecture.

What we are really building

The pornography use case is the wedge.

The real company is much larger.

We are building a behavioral operating system.

A system that can eventually protect:

sleep,

deep work,

exercise,

financial discipline,

relationships,

and other commitments users voluntarily choose.

The underlying engine is the same.

Detect a behavioral pattern.

Create friction.

Restore intentionality.

What we are not building

We are not building spyware.

We are not building parental surveillance software.

We are not building a morality engine.

We are not building an app that judges people.

Sentinel does not decide what users should avoid.

Users decide.

Sentinel enforces their own decisions.

The standard

Every feature should pass one test.

Will a user’s future self be grateful that this feature existed?

If the answer is no,

we should not build it.

The long game

I want Sentinel to become infrastructure.

The same way antivirus software protects computers,

Sentinel should protect human agency.

Imagine a world where your devices help you become the person you intended to become.

Not by controlling you.

By protecting commitments you consciously made.

That is a meaningful technology company.

The founder rule

Never optimize for engagement.

Never optimize for addiction.

Never optimize for data collection.

Optimize for promises kept.

The company wins when users need us less,

not more.

That is unusual.

That is difficult.

That is the point.

The metric

Revenue matters.

Retention matters.

Accuracy matters.

But the real metric is simpler.

How many times did Sentinel prevent a decision that the user would later regret?

If we can increase that number at scale,

we will have built something that genuinely improves lives.

That is worth building.

ethical-boundaries.md

Sentinel ethical boundaries

Version: 1.0

Status: Constitutional document

Priority: Permanent

Executive summary

Sentinel is built to protect user autonomy.

The same technologies that can help people maintain self-control can also be used for surveillance, coercion, manipulation, and abuse.

This document defines the ethical boundaries that Sentinel will not cross.

These are not marketing statements.

These are product constraints.

If a feature violates these principles, the feature should not be built.

The purpose of Sentinel

Sentinel exists to help users keep commitments they voluntarily make to themselves.

The product is fundamentally about self-directed protection.

Not external control.

The primary ethical principle

The user is the principal.

Sentinel acts on behalf of the user’s explicitly chosen goals.

Sentinel does not create goals for the user.

What Sentinel will never become

Secret monitoring software

Sentinel will never be designed for covert surveillance.

It will never secretly observe another person’s device.

It will never hide its activity from the device owner.

Protection must always be visible.

Partner surveillance

Sentinel will never provide tools for:

monitoring a spouse,

monitoring a partner,

monitoring a friend,

monitoring an employee,

monitoring another adult without their explicit consent.

This includes:

screen monitoring,

content reporting,

activity tracking,

remote observation,

behavioral reporting.

Screenshot sharing

Sentinel will never automatically share screenshots with:

partners,

parents,

employers,

religious leaders,

coaches,

therapists,

or Sentinel itself.

The visual content of a user’s screen belongs to the user.

Cloud screen analysis

Sentinel will never require cloud processing of screen content for core protection.

On-device analysis is a foundational principle.

Cloud features may exist in the future only if they do not require uploading private screen content.

Behavioral advertising

Sentinel will never use behavioral data to target advertisements.

No ad network integration.

No attention optimization.

No engagement maximization.

The business model must remain aligned with user wellbeing.

Addiction optimization

Sentinel will never optimize for:

daily app opens,

screen time,

engagement loops,

compulsive streaks,

or psychological dependency.

A successful Sentinel user may use the app less over time.

That is acceptable.

Informed consent

Users must understand:

what Sentinel can see,

what Sentinel cannot see,

what Sentinel stores,

what Sentinel never stores,

how interventions work,

how to disable protection,

what data leaves the device.

Permissions should never be obtained through deception.

Reversible consent

Users must be able to withdraw consent.

Protection may include friction.

It must not include imprisonment.

Emergency override exists because autonomy remains the highest value.

The autonomy test

Before building a feature, ask:

Does this increase the user’s ability to act according to their own values?

If the answer is no,

the feature should not be built.

The coercion test

Ask:

Would this feature become harmful if used by an abusive partner, controlling parent, authoritarian employer, or coercive institution?

If yes,

the feature requires extreme scrutiny.

Accountability features

Future accountability features must follow these rules.

Opt-in only

Explicitly configured

Easily understandable

Metadata only

Never screenshot based

An accountability partner may know that an intervention occurred.

They should not know what was on the screen.

Children and parental control

Sentinel is not designed primarily as parental surveillance software.

Future family features must prioritize:

transparency,

consent,

age appropriateness,

and privacy.

The architecture should not become generalized spyware.

AI boundaries

The AI should answer narrowly scoped questions.

Examples

Is explicit content visible?

Has a protection rule been triggered?

The AI should not infer:

political beliefs,

religious beliefs,

sexual orientation,

mental health diagnoses,

relationship quality,

or personal identity.

Emotional boundaries

Sentinel should never intentionally induce:

shame,

fear,

dependency,

guilt,

or humiliation.

Interventions should be calm and respectful.

The goal is recovery.

Data minimization

Collect the minimum information necessary for protection.

If a feature can be built without collecting additional data,

it should be.

Future usefulness is not sufficient justification for data collection.

Transparency

Users should be able to inspect:

permissions,

protection status,

privacy status,

stored data,

intervention history,

and network activity.

Opaque systems do not deserve deep permissions.

Open verification

The privacy architecture should be independently verifiable.

Where possible:

open-source components,

security audits,

privacy audits,

transparency reports,

and reproducible claims.

Trust should be earned through architecture,

not promises.

Founder responsibility

The founding team has a special responsibility.

Screen access creates asymmetric power.

Power tends to expand.

This document exists to resist that expansion.

The hard questions

Before any major feature launch, ask:

Does this help users become more free?

Does this respect their dignity?

Would we want this feature on our own phones?

Could this feature be abused?

Can we achieve the same outcome with less data?

What would the worst actor do with this capability?

The constitutional amendment rule

Changing this document should require:

explicit documentation,

written justification,

founder approval,

and public transparency.

Ethical boundaries should be difficult to change.

The final principle

Sentinel exists to increase human agency.

If Sentinel ever becomes a system that primarily increases the agency of someone other than the user,

it has failed its mission.

The product should always leave users with **more ownership over their lives,

not less**.

That is the boundary.

That is the promise.

That is the constitution.

privacy-security.md

Sentinel privacy and security architecture

Version: 1.0

Status: Critical design document

Priority: Highest

Executive summary

Sentinel requires one of the most sensitive permissions available on a smartphone: the ability to observe screen content.

This creates a trust problem that is more important than model accuracy, feature count, or growth.

The privacy architecture must therefore be designed around a single objective:

Sentinel must not be capable of becoming surveillance software.

The system should be architected so that the company, developers, and servers cannot view a user’s screen, recover historical screen content, or reconstruct private activity.

Privacy is not a policy.

Privacy is an architectural constraint.

Threat model

Sentinel must protect users against four classes of risk.

External attackers

Hackers attempting to obtain screen content.

Internal abuse

Employees or developers accessing user data.

Government or legal requests

Requests for stored screen content.

Future company changes

Business incentives that could encourage data collection.

The architecture should minimize all four.

The core privacy principle

Eyes without memory.

Sentinel may observe the current screen frame for the purpose of classification.

It must not retain the frame.

The frame exists only long enough to produce a confidence score.

Then it is destroyed.

Data flow

↓

RAM

↓

TensorFlow Lite inference

↓

Confidence score

↓

Frame deleted

No disk write.

No cache.

No upload.

No backup.

No cloud storage.

The privacy guarantee

Sentinel should be able to truthfully say:

We do not store screenshots.

We do not upload screenshots.

We do not record screen activity.

We do not build a browsing history.

We do not know what content you viewed.

We do not know which explicit images triggered interventions.

We cannot recover your screen history.

This should be technically true.

On-device processing

All AI inference occurs locally.

Requirements

No cloud inference.

No remote API calls.

No image transmission.

No background synchronization of visual data.

Internet access is unnecessary for the MVP.

The app should function completely offline.

privacy-security.md

Sentinel privacy and security architecture

Version: 1.0

Status: Critical design document

Priority: Highest

Executive summary

Sentinel requires one of the most sensitive permissions available on a smartphone: the ability to observe screen content.

This creates a trust problem that is more important than model accuracy, feature count, or growth.

The privacy architecture must therefore be designed around a single objective:

Sentinel must not be capable of becoming surveillance software.

The system should be architected so that the company, developers, and servers cannot view a user’s screen, recover historical screen content, or reconstruct private activity.

Privacy is not a policy.

Privacy is an architectural constraint.

Threat model

Sentinel must protect users against four classes of risk.

External attackers

Hackers attempting to obtain screen content.

Internal abuse

Employees or developers accessing user data.

Government or legal requests

Requests for stored screen content.

Future company changes

Business incentives that could encourage data collection.

The architecture should minimize all four.

The core privacy principle

Eyes without memory.

Sentinel may observe the current screen frame for the purpose of classification.

It must not retain the frame.

The frame exists only long enough to produce a confidence score.

Then it is destroyed.

ai-system.md

Sentinel AI system architecture

Version: 1.0

Status: AI engineering specification

Primary objective: Real-time explicit content detection with privacy-first on-device inference

Executive summary

The Sentinel AI system is responsible for detecting explicit sexual content on a user’s screen in real time and triggering behavioral interventions with extremely low latency.

The AI system is not a general-purpose vision model.

It is a specialized safety classifier optimized for:

speed,

privacy,

battery efficiency,

low false positives,

on-device execution.

The defining principle of the AI system is:

All visual analysis happens locally on the device.

No screenshots are uploaded.

No screenshots are stored.

No cloud inference is performed.

Design philosophy

Most AI products optimize for intelligence.

Sentinel optimizes for trust.

A slightly less accurate model that users trust is more valuable than a highly accurate model that feels invasive.

Therefore the AI system is designed around three priorities.

Priority 1

Privacy

Priority 2

Reliability

Priority 3

Accuracy

The AI responsibility boundary

The AI only answers one question.

“Is explicit sexual content currently visible on the screen?”

The AI does not:

identify people,

recognize faces,

read messages,

interpret conversations,

analyze personal photos,

infer user identity,

generate descriptions,

store observations.

The output is simply a probability estimate.

mvp-spec.md

Sentinel MVP specification

Version: 1.0

Status: Build specification

Target platform: Android

Development priority: Highest

MVP objective

Build the smallest possible product that proves the core hypothesis:

Real-time AI intervention can prevent users from consuming explicit content they intended to avoid.

The MVP is successful if users consistently report that Sentinel interrupted a pornography session before completion.

Non-goals

The MVP is not:

a productivity suite,

a habit tracker,

a meditation app,

a parental control app,

a social network,

an accountability platform.

Every feature must support the single objective of behavior interruption.

Primary user

A user who wants to quit pornography and is willing to give the app strong permissions in exchange for strong protection.

User promise

“When explicit content appears on my phone, Sentinel will intervene before I continue.”

Core experience

Setup

User installs the app.

User grants permissions.

User selects protection settings.

Protection becomes active.

Intervention

User encounters explicit content.

Sentinel detects it.

The screen is immediately obscured.

The user enters a cooldown state.

The user completes a recovery action.

Access is restored after the cooldown.

Success criteria

Primary metric

Interrupted explicit content sessions.

Definition:

An intervention occurs and the user does not return to explicit content within the next 10 minutes.

Secondary metrics

Intervention latency

False positive rate

False negative rate

Daily active users

7-day retention

Average cooldown completion rate

Permission retention

Functional requirements

FR-1 Screen monitoring

Sentinel monitors visible screen content while protection is active.

Requirements:

monitor foreground app,

analyze screen frames locally,

operate continuously,

minimize battery usage.

Target frame rate:

1 frame per second initially.

Future target:

2 to 4 FPS.

FR-2 Explicit content detection

The AI classifier outputs:

Safe

Possibly explicit

Explicit

Only Explicit triggers immediate intervention in V1.

FR-3 Immediate intervention

When explicit content is detected:

Display full-screen black overlay.

Vibrate device.

Pause interaction.

Start cooldown timer.

Prevent access to the triggering app during cooldown.

Latency target:

Less than 800 milliseconds.

FR-4 Cooldown timer

Default:

10 minutes.

Configurable:

5 minutes

10 minutes

15 minutes

20 minutes

The timer cannot be skipped.

FR-5 Recovery task

The user must complete one recovery action.

V1 options:

20 push-ups

2-minute breathing exercise

5-minute walk

The task should be selectable during onboarding.

FR-6 Lock periods

Users choose a protection duration.

Options:

2 hours

8 hours

24 hours

3 days

7 days

During the lock period:

Protection cannot be casually disabled.

FR-7 Emergency override

One emergency override is allowed.

Requirements:

60-second countdown

Clear warning

No immediate reactivation

This prevents panic situations while preserving friction.

problem.md

The problem Sentinel solves

Version: 0.1

Status: Product foundation

Executive summary

People do not usually fail because they lack awareness.

They fail because they lose control during short periods of emotional activation.

Existing blockers focus on access control.

Sentinel focuses on behavior interruption.

That is the core distinction.

The modern attention environment

Smartphones have become environments of continuous behavioral stimulation.

Infinite scrolling

Algorithmic recommendations

Autoplay

Short-form video

Push notifications

Instant access to explicit content

Instant access to gambling

Instant access to shopping

Instant access to distraction

The device is optimized for immediate engagement.

The user is trying to optimize for long-term goals.

These incentives are fundamentally misaligned.

The failure of willpower

Most digital wellbeing products are built on a flawed assumption:

“If users really want to stop, they will stop.”

Behavioral psychology suggests the opposite.

Willpower is:

state dependent,

fatigue dependent,

emotion dependent,

context dependent.

A decision made at 8 AM is not the same decision made at 1 AM.

The relapse loop

A simplified relapse loop looks like this.

Trigger

↓

Attention

↓

Curiosity

↓

Search

↓

Escalation

↓

Consumption

↓

Regret

Most blockers only affect Search.

Sentinel targets Escalation.

easy to configure,

effective for known websites.

Weaknesses:

ineffective inside apps,

ineffective for social media feeds.

vision.md

Sentinel Vision

Version: 0.1

Status: Founding Vision Document

The Idea

Sentinel exists because human beings are often forced to rely on willpower in environments specifically designed to defeat willpower.

Modern smartphones are extraordinary machines for capturing attention. Infinite feeds, algorithmic recommendations, autoplay, notifications, and instant access create a system where the easiest action is rarely the most important one.

Most productivity tools assume that people fail because they lack discipline.

We believe people often fail because they lack friction.

Sentinel is a friction engine.

The Fundamental Insight

There are two versions of every user.

The Rational Self

The person who:

wants to study,

wants to exercise,

wants to sleep,

wants to avoid pornography,

wants to build a meaningful life,

installs blockers,

creates goals,

makes commitments.

The Impulsive Self

The person who appears during:

loneliness,

boredom,

stress,

fatigue,

anxiety,

late-night scrolling,

emotional discomfort.

These two selves make different decisions.

The tragedy is that the impulsive self often has full administrative control over the device.

Sentinel changes that.

The Commitment Device

Economists and behavioral scientists have long studied commitment devices.

A commitment device is a mechanism that restricts future behavior in order to protect long-term goals.

Examples:

locking money away,

deleting social media,

giving someone else the password,

setting irreversible deadlines.

Sentinel is a digital commitment device.

The user voluntarily gives Sentinel permission to protect decisions made during moments of clarity.

The Critical Window

Most compulsive behaviors contain a narrow window during which intervention is highly effective.

The sequence often looks like:

Trigger

↓

Attention

↓

Exploration

↓

Escalation

↓

Compulsion

↓

Regret

Traditional blockers intervene at the beginning.

Sentinel intervenes during exploration and escalation, when the behavior is becoming self-reinforcing.

This is the window where a 30-second interruption can change the outcome.

Why Pornography First

Pornography is the initial use case for three reasons.

High Frequency

Many users encounter triggering content daily.

Cross-Platform

Explicit content appears in browsers, social media, messaging apps, image galleries, and embedded media.

Strong Impulse Dynamics

Pornography consumption often involves rapid transitions from intention to behavior.

This makes it an ideal environment for testing real-time intervention.

The Product Is Not Moral

Sentinel is not a moral authority.

Sentinel does not decide what users should avoid.

The user decides.

Sentinel enforces the user’s own commitments.

This distinction is critical.

The product should never shame the user.

It should assist the user.

The Psychological Model

Sentinel is built on five assumptions.

1. Impulses Are Temporary

The intensity of an urge usually decreases with time.

2. Friction Changes Behavior

Even small delays can significantly reduce impulsive actions.

3. State Matters More Than Character

People make different decisions when tired, stressed, or emotionally activated.

4. Recovery Is Better Than Punishment

Interruptions should help users regain control, not induce guilt.

5. Trust Is Essential

Users will only grant deep device permissions if Sentinel earns extraordinary trust.

The Trust Equation

Trust is more important than AI accuracy.

A 99% accurate model that feels invasive will fail.

An 85% accurate model that feels private and respectful can succeed.

Trust is created through:

transparency,

local processing,

user control,

predictable behavior,

explainable interventions,

minimal data collection.

Eyes Without Memory

The defining privacy principle is:

Sentinel has eyes but no memory.

This means:

screen frames are analyzed locally,

frames are immediately discarded,

no screenshots are stored,

no screenshots are uploaded,

no cloud analysis,

no remote model inference,

no behavioral surveillance.

The AI observes the present moment only.

The Emotional Experience

Sentinel should not feel like parental control software.

It should feel like a calm, competent guardian.

When an intervention occurs, the emotional tone should be:

calm,

respectful,

confident,

nonjudgmental.

Never:

angry,

disappointed,

manipulative,

guilt-inducing.

The interface should communicate:

“You asked me to protect this commitment.”

From Blocker to Guardian

A blocker says:

“You cannot access this.”

A guardian says:

“I know what you’re trying to protect.”

That difference changes the entire product.

The Long-Term Vision

Sentinel eventually becomes an operating layer above the operating system.

It understands goals, commitments, and vulnerabilities.

Examples:

protect sleep,

protect deep work,

protect relationships,

protect sobriety,

protect financial discipline,

protect physical health.

The AI becomes increasingly personalized.

Not through surveillance.

Through consent-based behavioral understanding.

Predictive Protection

The final evolution is not reactive blocking.

It is predictive protection.

Sentinel recognizes patterns such as:

time of day,

app sequences,

scrolling duration,

emotional indicators,

previous intervention history.

It intervenes before the impulse reaches peak intensity.

Example:

“You’ve been scrolling for 27 minutes. This pattern has led to a relapse 8 times in the past month. Activate Focus Mode?”

This is fundamentally different from a website blocker.

The Final Form

The final version of Sentinel is a behavioral firewall.

Just as antivirus software protects devices from malicious software,

Sentinel protects human attention from destructive behavioral loops.

The operating system protects the phone.

Sentinel protects the person using the phone.

The Standard

Every feature must pass this test:

Does this help the user become more aligned with the life they consciously want to live?

If the answer is no,

we do not build it.

Sentinel

The self-control operating system

Version: 0.1 (Founding Document)

Vision

Sentinel is an AI-powered commitment device that helps people keep promises they make to themselves.

Most digital wellbeing tools fail because they rely on willpower during moments when willpower is weakest. Website blockers can be disabled. App limits can be ignored. Content filters only work in browsers. The phone remains optimized for impulse.

Sentinel changes the model.

Instead of blocking websites, Sentinel responds to what is actually happening on the screen. It detects explicit sexual content, compulsive scrolling, and other predefined triggers in real time and intervenes immediately with a structured interruption designed to break the impulse cycle.

The goal is not punishment.

The goal is to create enough friction between urge and action that the rational version of the user regains control.

Mission

Build the world’s most trusted AI guardian for digital self-control.

Sentinel should eventually become a behavioral firewall for smartphones, computers, and wearable devices, protecting users from behaviors they have consciously chosen to avoid.

Core Principle

Increase friction during impulses and reduce friction during goals.

Every feature must satisfy this principle.

The Problem

Current blockers fail for four reasons:

They block websites, not screen content.

They are easy to disable.

They react too late.

They do not interrupt the psychological momentum of an impulse.

Sentinel focuses on the critical 2 to 10 minute window during which many compulsive behaviors occur.

The First Use Case

The initial product focuses on pornography interruption.

When explicit content appears on the screen, Sentinel immediately:

obscures the content,

interrupts the session,

starts a mandatory cooldown timer,

guides the user through a predefined recovery action.

This creates a buffer between temptation and behavior.

Product Philosophy

Sentinel is not a surveillance app.

Sentinel is a private on-device guardian.

The AI has eyes but no memory.

Screen analysis happens locally on the device.

No screenshots are uploaded.

No screenshots are stored.

No browsing history is transmitted.

No personal content is used to train remote models.

Privacy is not a feature.

Privacy is the foundation of the product.

Why This Matters

People frequently install blockers during moments of clarity and uninstall them during moments of weakness.

Sentinel exists to preserve the decisions made by the user’s rational self.

The product should feel like a trusted guardian standing between the user and a destructive impulse.

The Long-Term Vision

Today:

pornography interruption,

app locking,

cooldowns.

Tomorrow:

doomscrolling prevention,

gambling interruption,

shopping impulse control,

sleep protection,

study enforcement,

exercise accountability,

personalized behavioral intervention,

cross-device commitment contracts.

The final vision is a self-control operating system that helps users align their daily actions with their long-term goals.

MVP

The first version will be Android-only and include:

real-time screen monitoring,

on-device NSFW detection,

black-screen intervention,

configurable cooldown timer,

mandatory recovery task,

lock periods,

local-only processing,

basic analytics.

Success Metric

The primary metric is interrupted relapse events.

Sentinel succeeds when a user reaches for a behavior they intended to avoid and the product creates enough friction that the behavior does not occur.

Guiding Constraint

Every design decision should answer one question:

Does this increase the probability that the user keeps a promise they made to themselves?

If the answer is no, the feature does not belong in Sentinel.
